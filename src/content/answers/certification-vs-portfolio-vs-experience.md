## Short answer

They prove different things, so they aren't interchangeable:

- **A certification** proves you passed an exam — real signal of vocabulary and breadth, zero signal of whether you can build.
- **A portfolio** proves you built something — but reviewers can't tell from a repo alone whether it worked, so the write-up and measured results matter as much as the code.
- **Experience** proves your work survived real users, real data, and real incidents — the strongest signal and the only one you can't shortcut.

## When each one wins

**Choose a certification when** the roles you're targeting list one by name (common in cloud and enterprise jobs), when you're switching fields and need a vocabulary floor fast, or when your employer's review process credits it. Pick from the [certification registry](/certifications) — every entry there is verified against the official page, with the LMVersity tracks that map to its objectives.

**Choose portfolio work when** you're an engineer and the job is about building. One shipped feature — with evals, logging, and a write-up of what broke — outweighs a shelf of course certificates. The [role paths](/roles) end in exactly this kind of evidence.

**Choose experience-shaped evidence when** you can get it: an AI feature at your current job, a real tool for a real user, freelance or volunteer work with actual usage. If no job offers it yet, a project with real users (even a handful) is closer to experience than another certificate is.

## The mistake to avoid

Collecting credentials as a substitute for building. Three certifications and zero shipped things reads as avoidance, not ambition. The ordering that works: learn → build → *then* certify if the job market you're entering filters on it — never certify instead of build.
