LMV is the short name for LMVersity, a free, hands-on curriculum for learning AI engineering at lmversity.com. It teaches the path from a first prompt to agents running in production: how language models work, prompting and context engineering, retrieval, evaluation, agent harnesses, and a complete Forward Deployed Engineer path. No paywall, no account, no certificate.

## The short version

- **LMV = LMVersity.** Say it letter by letter. The three-letter mark in the corner of every page is the same name.
- **LM is language models**, the thing the whole era is built on and worth understanding one layer below the API you call.
- **Versity is the shape**: a structured course of study in dependency order, not a pile of tutorials.
- **It is free and open.** Every page can be read without an account. There is no paid tier.
- **It is not accredited** and does not issue certificates, on purpose. The point is what you can build afterwards.
- **It is not "light motor vehicle".** Same letters, different world.

## What LMV actually is

LMVersity is a website of lessons, guides, quizzes, scenarios and interview questions about building software with large language models. The material is written for people who can already get a model to do something interesting in a chat window and now want to know what happens when that becomes a feature with real users, real data, real permissions and a real budget.

The curriculum is grouped into technical tracks, ordered so that each one builds on the last. The early tracks cover how language models work and how to prompt them well. The middle covers retrieval, tool use, and evaluation. The later tracks cover agents, the harnesses that make them safe to run, and the operational work of keeping them running. The full map is on the [curriculum page](/learn).

Alongside the tracks there are role paths, which pick the lessons relevant to one job and skip the rest. The most complete of these is the [Forward Deployed Engineer path](/roles/forward-deployed-engineer), a standalone nine-month programme with its own labs, drills, bootcamps and capstones.

## Why the name

Two parts. LM is language models: the technology this whole era is built on, and the thing worth understanding one layer deeper than the API you call. Versity is the shape of what is here, a structured course of study that starts at the foundations and ends in production. Put together, LMVersity. Shortened, LMV.

A university it is not. There is no accreditation, no admission, no fee and no certificate. What the site keeps from a university is the sequencing; what it drops is everything else. The longer version of that reasoning is on the [About page](/about).

## How LMV is different from a course platform

Most courses teach the path where everything works, then leave you at the edge of it. LMVersity is organised the other way round, around the cases that do not fit: the table that lost its headers during chunking, the agent that goes quiet at 3am because a token expired and nothing refreshed it, the eval score that climbed while quality fell. Each technique is documented with its failure modes, not only its happy path.

A few editorial rules hold across the site. Statistics appear only where a named source exists. Where providers or tools differ, the difference is stated rather than smoothed over. Where the evidence is thin, the page says so. Content is Markdown in a public repository, so any claim can be traced through its edit history.
