## The situation

You are building a research feature that searches a private knowledge base, compares sources, and writes a short brief. Users want to see progress, but partial answers can be mistaken for final conclusions.

## Constraints

- Runs take minutes, not seconds — no feedback at all reads as a hung page, and abandonment mid-run wastes the spend.
- Anything rendered is a screenshot waiting to happen: a partial finding shown without a label will be quoted as a conclusion.
- Sources are external and can disappear or conflict mid-run; the final brief must trace to source IDs, not to prose that mentioned them.
- Cancellation has to stop downstream spend, not just the animation.

## Options on the table

**Stream raw model tokens.** Rejected: token streams are unlabeled by nature — a half-formed sentence about a source looks exactly like a finding, and there is no place to hang a caveat.

**Spinner until done.** Rejected: a multi-minute task with no visible work invites abandonment and gives the operator no diagnostic surface when it fails.

**Typed progress events.** Chosen: the UI renders stage, status, and labeled intermediate artifacts; the final brief is a separate object that only exists after validation.

## The first decision

Stream typed progress events rather than an unstructured stream of model prose. Show stages such as searching, selecting sources, drafting, and checking citations. Label partial findings and keep the final brief separate.

## Guided reasoning

1. Define a state machine for queued, running, waiting, completed, failed, and cancelled.
2. Give each stage a time and cost budget.
3. Store source IDs and intermediate artifacts so the final answer is traceable.
4. Make cancellation stop downstream work and revoke temporary authority.
5. Validate the final structure and citations before marking the task complete.

Streaming improves perceived responsiveness; it does not make an unsupported claim safe. Combine [streaming responses](/learn/genai-app-dev/streaming-responses-to-the-ui) with [evaluating RAG quality](/learn/rag/evaluating-rag-quality).

## What went wrong

An early build streamed citation links into the draft as the model produced them, before validation ran. A source URL that no longer resolved sat on screen, styled identically to verified ones, for the rest of the run — and into the exported brief, because the export copied the rendered text. The postmortem produced two rules that cost little and save much: intermediate artifacts render with their stage label attached, and citations are only styled as citations after they validate. The failure was not the missing source; it was the UI lying about how much checking had happened.

## Failure injection

- Kill a source mid-retrieval so its citation arrives as a 404 after the draft already referenced it.
- Stall the token stream for thirty seconds — the UI must show a stalled run, not a finished-looking one.
- Cancel during a tool call and confirm the tool result arriving afterwards is discarded, not appended.
- Feed two sources that flatly contradict each other and check the draft shows the conflict instead of picking one.
- Validate a citation after the run ends and confirm the export re-checks rather than trusting the rendered text.

## What to test first

Test cancellation during retrieval, a source that disappears, conflicting sources, a partial network disconnect, a citation validation failure, and a user navigating away mid-run.

## Variants

- **Startup:** one researcher, one queue — the whole system can be an agent loop plus a spreadsheet of sources; the discipline is keeping claims attached to citations.
- **Enterprise:** research feeds downstream consumers — output contracts matter more than speed: what does the report guarantee about source coverage, and what does "the agent checked" actually mean.
- **Regulated:** sources need provenance and retention — a cited claim must resolve to the version that was read, which means archiving sources at fetch time, not linking to live pages.
- **Low-connectivity / long-horizon:** long research runs fail mid-way — resumable checkpoints and partial-result quality (what do you show when 60% done) become the design.

## Two ways to run this

**Whiteboard mode** — read only the situation and constraints, then stop. Sketch your options, decision, and failure plan before opening the rest; the scenario's choices become a grading key for your reasoning, not just a story.

**Guided mode** — read straight through. The options and guided-reasoning sections walk the decision step by step; use them to compare your instinct with the documented one.

## Evaluation rubric

A strong answer keeps citations attached to claims end to end, distinguishes "searched" from "verified," and degrades gracefully when sources disagree or run out. Weak answers stream confident prose with sources bolted on after, count pages fetched as coverage, or let the synthesis layer silently drop the inconvenient source.
