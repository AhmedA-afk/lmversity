## The situation

A clinic's appointment line runs a voice agent: patients call to reschedule. Callers report the agent "doesn't listen" — it answers too slowly, then keeps talking when they try to correct it. The vendor dashboard shows healthy per-stage latency. The team suspects the model but has no data.

## Constraints

- Patients skew older; the agent must handle slow, quiet, accented, and code-mixed speech — a system tuned on demo audio fails this population.
- Appointment changes touch real scheduling — a misheard date is a missed appointment, which is a medical-operations problem, not a UX nit.
- The pipeline is cascaded (ASR → LLM → TTS); each stage is a different vendor, so "the vendor is slow" isn't a single answer.
- The clinic can't change phone numbers or add an app requirement — the fix has to work on the existing call path.

## What you don't know

- Where the silence budget actually goes — ASR endpointing delay, LLM first token, TTS first byte, or transport.
- Whether "talking over people" is missing barge-in, a barge-in that fires too late, or barge-in that fires on background noise and then gets disabled.
- Whether misheard dates come from ASR errors, confirmation-skipping, or the LLM inventing plausible dates.
- What the endpointing threshold is — too aggressive and it cuts callers off mid-sentence; too lax and the agent feels dead.

## Options on the table

**Swap to a speech-to-speech model.** Tempting marketing fix; rejected as first move — it changes every variable at once and the team hasn't measured which variable is broken.

**Tune the existing cascade.** Chosen: measure the silence budget per stage on real calls, fix turn-taking mechanics (endpointing + barge-in + confirmation design), then evaluate whether any vendor swap is still needed.

**Add "press 1 to speak" turn-taking.** Rejected: it solves overlap by destroying the conversational feel — the population being served needs natural speech, not a walkie-talkie.

## The first decision

Instrument before tuning. Add per-stage timestamps to live calls (endpointing delay, ASR-final, LLM-first-token, TTS-first-byte) and per-event logs for barge-in triggers and false triggers — the current state is "healthy latency" aggregates that hide the turn-taking failures entirely.

## Guided reasoning

1. Measure the silence budget per stage on real calls, sliced by caller type — the aggregate may look fine while accented speech waits twice as long for ASR finals.
2. Find the endpointing setting; test it against the actual patient population's pause patterns.
3. Verify barge-in exists and works: is VAD active during TTS playback? Does a trigger actually cancel synthesis and the in-flight LLM stream?
4. Fix date handling: heard-dates get read-back confirmation ("Thursday the 19th — is that right?"), not silent acceptance — a confirmation turn is cheaper than a missed appointment.
5. Re-measure: silence budget, interruption success rate, date-error rate — all three move together or something's still wrong.

## What went wrong

The instrumentation found three stacked problems: endpointing was tuned to a 700ms silence threshold that cut off slow speakers mid-thought; barge-in existed but VAD was muted during TTS playback (the vendor's default), so "stop, that's wrong" went unheard; and ASR confidence on dates was high enough to skip confirmation even though roughly a sixth of date errors came through as confidently-wrong finals. The fixes were all configuration and design, not vendors: endpointing lengthened with a filler-word detector, playback-time VAD enabled with echo-aware triggering, and mandatory read-back on any date/time entity. Complaints dropped to noise level within two weeks.

## Failure injection

- Play a call with a slow, pause-heavy speaker and confirm endpointing doesn't cut them off.
- Inject background TV noise during TTS playback and verify barge-in doesn't false-trigger — or that a false trigger recovers gracefully.
- Feed a quietly-spoken ambiguous date and verify the read-back confirmation fires.
- Drop the ASR stream mid-utterance and check the agent apologizes-and-reasks rather than answering silence.

## Variants

- **Startup:** pick the managed speech API, accept the lock-in, and spend the design effort on the turn-taking UX — that's where the felt latency lives.
- **Enterprise:** telephony integration and call-recording compliance join the problem — the latency budget now includes SIP legs and legal-hold storage, and the vendor negotiation is part of the architecture.
- **Regulated:** voice is biometric data in some jurisdictions — consent, recording disclosure, and retention shape the pipeline before latency does.
- **Low-connectivity:** the network is the variance, not the mean — design for degraded mode (smaller models, barge-in tolerance, honest "repeat that" paths) because the p95 is the product.

## Evaluation rubric

A strong answer budgets latency across the whole turn (ASR, LLM, TTS, network), designs turn-taking and barge-in as UX features not afterthoughts, and measures p95 not mean. Weak answers optimise the model call and ignore the pipeline, treat silence gaps as unavoidable, or benchmark on a clean connection and ship the p50.
