## For interviewers: how to use these questions

Each question file is a topic hub — questions, concise answers, follow-ups, and a scoring section. Use them as a structure, not a script.

**The prompt pattern that works:** open with the question plainly ("what is prompt injection, and why is it hard to fix?"), then stay quiet. The follow-ups are where the signal lives — ask them when the candidate's answer is thin, rehearsed, or suspiciously smooth. A candidate who recites a polished definition and stalls on "why does that matter" has told you something real.

**Score the reasoning, not the recall.** The scoring sections name what strong and weak look like per topic. The pattern across all of them: strong answers connect the concept to an engineering consequence; weak answers recite the concept. You're testing whether they can *use* the idea, not whether they've read about it.

**Probe for the boundary.** The fastest way to depth-test an answer: ask "when is that wrong?" or "when would you choose the opposite?" Candidates who've done the work have a boundary; candidates who've memorized have a wall.

**Watch for the new formats.** The explain-trace, debug-output, choose-tradeoff, and defend-project questions test judgment under ambiguity — score the reasoning path, not the destination. A defensible wrong answer with sound reasoning usually beats a lucky right one.

## For candidates: how to practice

**Progressive hints, not answer-first reading.** Cover the answer; answer the question out loud in sixty seconds; then compare. The gap between your answer and the written one is the study material — read that part again. The follow-ups are the second pass: if the first answer was fluent but the follow-up stalled you, the stall is your next lesson.

**Answer out loud, on a timer.** Interview answers are a performance skill. Sixty seconds per question, spoken, is different from "I know this." The format questions especially — explain-trace and debug-output — need spoken practice because the skill is structuring a diagnosis in real time, not recalling a fact.

**Use the scoring sections as a self-check.** After answering, read "strong answers" and "weak answers" for the topic and place yourself honestly. The weak-answer descriptions are diagnostic — they name the specific shape of a memorized answer so you can catch yourself producing one.

**Follow the links back to curriculum.** Every answer links into lessons — the questions are an index into the material, not a substitute for it. A question you can't answer is a lesson to do, not a line to memorize.

## A note on both sides

The point of an interview question isn't the answer — it's the reasoning the question makes visible. Both sides get more from the follow-ups than the questions: the interviewer gets depth, the candidate gets to show how they think. Practice the follow-ups at least as hard as the questions.
