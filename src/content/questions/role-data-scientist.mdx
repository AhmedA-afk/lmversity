## What the role interview tests

Data science interviews test inference and experimentation judgment — and increasingly, whether you can work with generative systems: evaluating outputs you can't label cleanly, using LLMs as tools, and knowing when a model call beats a model build. Your advantage is rigor; the gap to close is the engineering surface.

## Mock set — junior

1. Maths foundations Q1–Q4 (probability, distributions, why correlation isn't causation)
2. Classical ML Q1–Q3 (metrics, leakage, baselines)
3. AI foundations Q1–Q3 (what generative models are and aren't)
4. Evals Q1–Q2 (what an eval is)
5. Defend-project on an analysis or model

*Pass bar:* statistical instinct intact, honest about the generative side being newer territory.

## Mock set — mid

1. Classical ML Q4–Q6 (when ML is the wrong tool)
2. Evals Q3–Q5 — designing evaluation for generative outputs (this is your leverage: it's experiment design)
3. LLM basics Q4–Q6 (failure modes, when to prompt vs fine-tune vs retrieve)
4. Choose-tradeoff: a metric that optimizes the wrong thing
5. Debug-output: an A/B result that contradicts the offline metric

*Pass bar:* treats LLM eval as a measurement problem — sampling, rater agreement, bias — not a vibes problem.

## Mock set — senior

1. Design-this-system: an evaluation framework for a generative product
2. Explain-trace: a model-quality regression after a prompt change — isolate cause from correlation
3. Choose-tradeoff: offline metrics vs online experiments for AI features
4. Classical ML — defend a simple analysis against an over-built model
5. Defend-project on a decision your analysis changed

*Pass bar:* they design the measurement before the model, and they're the person who catches the metric that lies.

## What interviewers probe for this role

- **Evaluation as experiment design.** LLM evals reward exactly your training — labeling schemes, inter-rater agreement, sampling bias, powered comparisons. Lead with it.
- **The honesty edge.** "The model can't answer that question" or "that metric doesn't measure the thing" is a senior DS answer.
- **Engineering breadth.** The common DS gap: production awareness. Know the observability and deployment basics even if they're not your depth.

## Project defense

Expect: how you validated, what the confidence in the result actually was, what you'd measure differently now — and for generative work, how you handled evaluation without clean labels.
