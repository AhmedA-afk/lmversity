## What the role interview tests

ML engineer interviews still weight fundamentals: classical ML, deep learning, and the math underneath — plus the engineering question of whether your models survive contact with production. LLM questions appear increasingly, but the differentiator is modeling judgment, not API fluency.

**Core topic hubs:** [Classical ML](/interview/classical-ml) · [Deep learning](/interview/deep-learning) · [Maths foundations](/interview/maths-foundations) · [Evals](/interview/evals) · [Production and observability](/interview/production-and-observability) · [AI system design](/interview/ai-system-design) · [AI foundations](/interview/ai-foundations)

## Mock set — junior

1. Classical ML Q1–Q3 (bias-variance, leakage, metrics)
2. Maths foundations Q1–Q3 (what a gradient is, why probability matters)
3. Deep learning Q1–Q3 (what a neural net learns, overfitting)
4. One debug-output: a training curve that plateaus — what do you check first
5. Defend-project on any modeling work

*Pass bar:* mechanics right, knows why leakage is the silent killer, can read a curve.

## Mock set — mid

1. Classical ML Q4–Q6 (feature engineering judgment, when not to use ML)
2. Deep learning Q4–Q6 (architecture choice, debugging training)
3. Evals — choose-tradeoff (precision vs recall for a real cost asymmetry)
4. Production Q3–Q5 (drift, monitoring, retraining triggers)
5. Design-this-system: an ML feature end to end

*Pass bar:* picks baselines before architectures, quantifies before optimizing, treats data quality as the real problem.

## Mock set — senior

1. Design-this-system: ML platform or a high-stakes model with regulatory exposure
2. Production — explain-trace on a model degrading in prod
3. Classical ML — defend a "boring" model choice against a fashionable one
4. Evals — design validation for a shifting distribution
5. Defend-project on a model that failed and what the postmortem found

*Pass bar:* skepticism as a default, infrastructure awareness, and the honesty to say "ML is the wrong tool here."

## What interviewers probe for this role

- **Baseline discipline.** Strong candidates reach for the simple model first and justify complexity — weak ones reach for the architecture.
- **Data over model.** The senior signal is treating data quality, labeling, and leakage as the engineering problem.
- **Production realism.** Training curves are easy; drift, skew, and monitoring are the job.

## Project defense

Expect: why this model class, how you validated, what the leakage surface was, what you'd monitor in week one, and what a 10% metric drop would make you check first.
