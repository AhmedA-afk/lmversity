## What the role interview tests

AI PM interviews test whether you can scope and sequence products built on systems that are *sometimes wrong*: what to automate vs assist, how to set quality bars you can't fully specify, and how to talk about capability without either overclaiming or dismissing. Technical depth isn't the bar — calibrated judgment is.

## Mock set — junior

1. AI foundations Q1–Q4 (capabilities, limits, why demos mislead)
2. LLM basics Q1–Q3 (plain-language mechanics)
3. Evals Q1–Q2 (why "it worked in the demo" isn't a quality bar)
4. Behavioral — a product decision under uncertainty
5. Defend-project on any shipped feature

*Pass bar:* accurate plain-language mental models, no capability overclaiming.

## Mock set — mid

1. Choose-tradeoff: automate vs assist for a described user workflow
2. Evals Q3–Q5 — how you'd set a launch bar for a non-deterministic feature
3. Safety Q1–Q3 (what injection and misuse mean for your product surface)
4. Design-this-system: scope an AI feature — what ships in v1 and why
5. Behavioral — a time the technology couldn't do what the roadmap promised

*Pass bar:* frames quality as measurable and probabilistic, sequences for learning not for demos.

## Mock set — senior

1. Design-this-system: an AI product strategy — where AI earns its place and where it's decoration
2. Choose-tradeoff: model cost vs quality vs the price the market bears
3. Behavioral — a launch that went wrong on AI failure modes; the org response
4. Safety — what guardrails are a product decision vs an engineering one
5. Defend-project on an AI bet you made and how it resolved

*Pass bar:* talks about trust calibration and failure UX as product surface; can say "don't build this with AI" credibly.

## What interviewers probe for this role

- **Calibration.** The failure mode is symmetric: hype ("AI will just do it") and dismissal ("it's just autocomplete"). Both fail. The strong answer knows what the tech does *at what reliability*.
- **Quality-bar thinking.** "How good is good enough to ship, measured how?" is the core AI-PM question — have a real framework.
- **Failure UX.** Non-deterministic products need designed failure paths, escalation UX, and trust repair — that's product work, and knowing it is the differentiator.

## Project defense

Expect: why AI was the right tool here, what the quality bar was and how you measured it, what the failure experience looked like for users, and what you'd de-scope today.
