## What the role interview tests

FDE interviews test the *deployment* skill set: entering an unfamiliar domain, scoping a problem the customer can't fully articulate, shipping something that works inside their constraints — their data, their security posture, their workflows. Technical depth matters, but the differentiator is ambiguity tolerance and judgment about what to build *first*.

## Mock set — junior

1. AI system design Q1–Q3 (components, where AI fits)
2. Production Q1–Q3 (why production AI differs from a demo)
3. Behavioral Q1–Q3 (a time you shipped under ambiguity)
4. One debug-output: an integration failing on customer data you've never seen
5. Defend-project on anything shipped to a real user

*Pass bar:* asks about constraints before proposing, comfortable saying "depends — on what?"

## Mock set — mid

1. Design-this-system: an AI feature for a described customer environment (sparse brief on purpose)
2. Safety — threat-model a deployment inside a customer's security perimeter
3. Behavioral — choose-tradeoff under a deadline with a watching client
4. Evals — how do you evaluate success when the customer can't define it
5. Explain-trace on a production incident at a customer site

*Pass bar:* scopes before building, surfaces the unstated constraint, treats the customer's definition of success as the spec.

## Mock set — senior

1. Design-this-system: first-90-days engagement — discovery, pilot, production, handoff
2. Behavioral — a deployment that failed; what the postmortem changed
3. Choose-tradeoff: customize vs generalize when one customer wants off-roadmap work
4. Production — observability for a system you don't fully control
5. Defend-project on the messiest thing you shipped

*Pass bar:* talks about stakeholders and adoption as readily as architecture; knows that the technical problem is usually the easy part.

## What interviewers probe for this role

- **Ambiguity as the medium.** They'll under-specify deliberately — the candidate who asks "what does success look like to *them*" is answering correctly.
- **Scope discipline.** FDE work dies of over-building; the strong answer ships the wedge first.
- **Customer-shaped engineering.** "Their SSO, their data residency, their ops team's skill level" — constraints arrive from outside, and the design absorbs them.
