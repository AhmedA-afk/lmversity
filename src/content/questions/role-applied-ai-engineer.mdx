## What the role interview tests

"Applied" means the interview weights *integration judgment*: picking the right technique for a product problem, shipping it behind real constraints, and knowing enough of each area to be dangerous in all of them. Breadth is the point — but they probe for a floor of depth everywhere.

## Mock set — junior

1. AI foundations Q1–Q3 (what models are, capabilities vs limits)
2. LLM basics Q1–Q3
3. RAG Q1–Q3
4. Structured outputs Q1–Q2
5. Defend-project on any shipped AI feature

*Pass bar:* accurate mechanics across the breadth, no bluffing at the edges.

## Mock set — mid

1. RAG Q4–Q6 (diagnosis, eval, chunking tradeoffs)
2. Evals Q3–Q5 (regression suites, sampling cost)
3. Production — choose-tradeoff (cost vs quality, cache vs freshness)
4. Safety Q1–Q3 (injection basics, why guardrails layer)
5. Design-this-system: a scoped AI feature

*Pass bar:* chooses techniques by constraint not fashion, names the eval before the architecture.

## Mock set — senior

1. Design-this-system: a multi-feature AI product surface
2. Explain-trace: a failing agentic workflow in production
3. Choose-tradeoff: build vs buy across model, framework, and eval stack
4. Safety — threat-model a feature with untrusted input
5. Defend-project on a cross-functional launch

*Pass bar:* product-shaped reasoning — they design for the user's failure modes, not the model's capabilities.

## What interviewers probe for this role

- **Technique-selection judgment.** "Why RAG and not fine-tuning, why a workflow and not an agent" — the *choosing* is the skill.
- **Shipping pragmatism.** The best answer is often the boring one that ships; flag when they'd prototype vs build properly.
- **Floor of depth.** Breadth candidates fail on depth checks — be solid on RAG internals and eval design specifically; those are where the probing concentrates.

## Project defense

Expect: why this technique for this problem, what you tried first and abandoned, how you evaluated without ground truth, and what the cost/latency/quality tradeoff looked like in numbers.
