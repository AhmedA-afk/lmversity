## The short answer

Pick one of the seven jobs below. Each card gives you a first prompt, the artifact you should walk away with, and the check that proves it is good. Do one today; the rest whenever the job shows up in your real work.

## How to run this lab

Every card follows the same loop: **task → prompt → artifact → check.** The artifact is the point — a real output you can use, not a chat transcript. The check is non-negotiable: if you skip it, you did an experiment about trust, not a workflow.

## The seven workflows

### 1. Research: brief me on something unfamiliar

- **Task:** get oriented on a topic you know nothing about — a regulation, a competitor, a technique.
- **First prompt:** "Explain {topic} to a smart beginner. What are the five things I must understand, what do people commonly get wrong, and what should I read next?"
- **Artifact:** a one-page brief in your own words, rewritten from the chat.
- **Check:** every factual claim you kept gets traced to a source you opened yourself. [Fact-checking an AI answer](/learn/ai-literacy/fact-check-an-ai-answer-step-by-step) is the method.

### 2. Writing: draft in your voice, not AI voice

- **Task:** a real piece of writing you owe someone — a tricky email, a status update, a proposal intro.
- **First prompt:** "Here are three paragraphs I wrote before: {paste}. Draft {the piece} in that voice. Ask me up to three questions first if anything is unclear."
- **Artifact:** the draft, edited until a colleague could not tell which parts were drafted.
- **Check:** read it aloud once. Anywhere you would not say it that way, rewrite it — that is your voice asserting itself.

### 3. Planning: turn a vague goal into a sequence

- **Task:** something you keep putting off because the first step is unclear — a move, an event, a career switch.
- **First prompt:** "Goal: {goal}. Constraints: {budget/time/people}. Give me a week-by-week plan with one decision I need to make per week, and flag the step most likely to be underestimated."
- **Artifact:** the plan with your real dates and names filled in — not the AI's placeholders.
- **Check:** the plan survives contact with your calendar. If week one is impossible, the plan was wrong, and you fix the plan, not your life.

### 4. Spreadsheet: get the formula and the shape

- **Task:** a real sheet problem — a budget, a tracker, a cleanup job.
- **First prompt:** "My sheet has columns {list them}. I want to {goal}. Give me the formula for one row, explain each part, and tell me what will break if a cell is empty."
- **Artifact:** a working formula in your actual sheet, plus the explanation saved in a cell comment.
- **Check:** paste the formula on a row where you already know the answer. It must reproduce your known result before it touches unknown data.

### 5. Document: restructure, don't just rephrase

- **Task:** a document that exists but does not work — too long, wrong order, wrong audience.
- **First prompt:** "This {document type} is for {audience} who needs to {decision}. Reorganize it so the decision comes first. Do not add facts; mark anything you remove."
- **Artifact:** the restructured document with a visible record of what moved and what was cut.
- **Check:** diff the old and new versions. Every removed sentence was either redundant or moved — nothing quietly disappeared.

### 6. Presentation: outline and speaker notes first

- **Task:** a talk or deck you have to give — team update, class, pitch.
- **First prompt:** "Talk: {topic} for {audience}, {minutes} minutes. Give me a slide-by-slide outline where each slide is one sentence, then write my speaker notes for the two hardest slides."
- **Artifact:** the one-sentence-per-slide outline plus real speaker notes you would actually say.
- **Check:** the outline tells the story without any slides. If the sentences alone do not flow, no template will save it.

### 7. Image: iterate on the description, not the hope

- **Task:** an image for a real use — a slide illustration, a card, a placeholder mockup.
- **First prompt:** describe the subject, the style, the mood, and what it must *not* contain. Then change one thing per round instead of re-rolling.
- **Artifact:** the final image plus the description that produced it, saved so you can do it again.
- **Check:** it does its job in context — readable at the size it will appear, nothing in it a viewer could mistake for real data or a real person.

## What you are actually practicing

Seven different jobs, one repeated skill: **describe the outcome precisely, keep the artifact, verify it yourself.** That loop is the whole [verification checklist](/learn/ai-literacy/the-verification-checklist) in miniature, and it is what separates using AI from being used by it.
