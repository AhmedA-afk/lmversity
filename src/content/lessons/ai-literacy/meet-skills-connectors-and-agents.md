## The short answer

A chatbot only talks. A **connector** lets it read or write inside one of your tools — email, calendar, files. A **skill** is a packaged recipe for doing a task the same way every time. An **agent** chains steps together and can act on your behalf. The safety question is never "is it smart?" It is "what can it touch, and what can it do there?"

## Why this matters

Every one of these looks like the same text box. The difference is the blast radius. A chatbot that writes a bad paragraph costs you a reread. An agent with permission to send email on your behalf can send the bad paragraph to your whole team before you see it.

Most AI mistakes people regret are not clever failures. They are permission failures: the tool was given access the task never needed.

## How it works

Think of the levels as adding capability, one at a time:

| Level | What it adds | Example | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chatbot | Answers from what you paste | "Summarize this report" | You over-share in the paste |
| + Connector | Read access to a tool | "Summarize today's unread email" | Sees more than the task needs |
| + Skill | A saved, repeatable recipe | "Use our meeting-notes format" | Recipe drifts out of date |
| + Agent | Acts across steps without you | "Find a slot and send invites" | Acts before you review |

The rule that scales across all four: **match the access to the task.** Reading your calendar to suggest a meeting time is reasonable. Writing to your calendar "just in case" is not.

## Three safety defaults

1. **Start read-only.** If a tool can look but not touch, use that mode first. Most tasks — summarize, find, compare, draft — only need reading.
2. **Review before send.** Anything that leaves your control — an email, a purchase, a post, a deletion — deserves a pause and a human check. Prefer tools that make this a step, not a setting you can forget.
3. **Keep the receipts.** Good agents show what they did: which tools they called, what they read, what they changed. If a tool cannot show its work, treat its results as unverified.

## A safe first experiment

Pick one low-stakes task — summarizing a folder of your own notes, or drafting a reply you will edit anyway. Connect only what the task needs, run it, and read the activity log afterward. You are testing two things: whether the output is good, and whether the access felt proportionate.
